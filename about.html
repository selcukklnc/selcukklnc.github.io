About

Researching AI-supported learning across educational contexts.

My academic work connects artificial intelligence in education, learning sciences, STEM education, software engineering, and design-based educational research.

Biography

I am a Postdoctoral Research Scholar at the University at Albany, State University of New York, working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, learning sciences, educational technology, and STEM education.

My research examines how AI-supported systems can help learners and educators engage in inquiry, reflection, data interpretation, computational thinking, and pedagogical decision-making. I am especially interested in generative AI, AI literacy, AI-TPACK, intelligent tutoring environments, and AI-supported assessment.

My interdisciplinary background in science education and software engineering shapes the way I approach educational technology. I study AI not simply as a technical tool, but as part of broader learning ecologies involving students, teachers, curriculum, assessment, institutional policy, and ethical decision-making.

Research identity

A central strand of my work involves designing AI-supported STEM learning environments that combine intelligent agents, real-time environmental data, and inquiry-based pedagogy. These designs include AI chatbot mentors, Arduino-based IoT sensor systems, and classroom activities that allow students to investigate authentic environmental and agricultural problems.

My scholarship has addressed AI literacy, AI-TPACK development among teachers, ethical AI assessment, generative AI in higher education, citizen science, distance education, and rural Ag-STEM learning. Across these projects, I aim to bridge theory, design, and classroom practice.

Current focus

  • Generative AI and AI literacy in higher education
  • AI-supported learning environments and chatbot tutors
  • AI-TPACK and teacher professional development
  • IoT-enabled citizen science and STEM learning
  • Assessment, ethics, and policy in AI in education
  • Computational thinking and data practices in K–12 STEM contexts
  • Mixed-methods and design-based research in technology-rich learning environments
